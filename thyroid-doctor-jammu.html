How thyroid problems are found and treated

The thyroid is a small gland in the neck that sets the pace of the body. Too little thyroid hormone slows things down; too much speeds them up. Neither can be judged from symptoms alone; the level of thyroid hormone is measured with a blood test, read together with the symptoms. A swelling or lump in the neck is a separate matter: it may need a scan even when the blood test is normal.

Treatment is usually long term, and the right dose can change with time. That is why thyroid care is a matter of regular follow-up rather than a single visit, and why it helps to see one doctor who knows your earlier reports.

Who should come

Anyone already on thyroid medicine who wants it reviewed. Anyone with complaints that can point to the thyroid: tiredness that does not lift, a change in weight without a reason, feeling cold or hot when others do not, hair fall, a swelling in the neck, or a fast or irregular heartbeat. And anyone with thyroid disease in the family who has not been tested.

What to bring

Your previous thyroid reports, in order if you can, and the thyroid medicine you take with its dose. Bring your other medicines too, because some affect the thyroid or its tests. Do not stop the medicine or change the dose on your own before the visit.

Thyroid with diabetes or blood pressure

Many patients have more than one long-term condition. An internal medicine physician looks at them together, so that the treatment for one does not work against another. The clinic also provides diabetes care and blood pressure and heart care.

Tests and medicines

Which blood tests are needed depends on your symptoms and your earlier reports. The clinic offers lab collection on site, and prescribed medicines are available from the in-house pharmacy immediately after the consultation.
