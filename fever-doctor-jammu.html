Fever and Infection Treatment in Jammu

Most fevers settle in a few days. Some do not, and those need a doctor to find the cause. Dr. Anil Kumar Dhar, an internal medicine physician (MBBS, MD, DNB), sees adults with fever and infections at his clinic on Canal Road, Bhagwati Nagar, Jammu.

How a fever is assessed

Fever is a sign, not a diagnosis. What matters is how long it has lasted, how it rises and falls, and what else you feel. When the cause is not clear from that, blood or urine tests may be needed; the clinic offers lab collection on site.

Treatment depends on the cause. Antibiotics work only against bacteria, so not every fever needs one. Prescribed medicines are available from the clinic's own pharmacy immediately after the consultation.

When to see the doctor

A fever that lasts more than two or three days, or one that goes and comes back. Fever with a cough or breathlessness, burning when you pass urine, a rash, or severe headache and body pain. And any fever in an older adult or in someone with diabetes, heart disease or lung disease, for whom an infection can turn serious faster.

What to bring

A note of your temperature readings and when you took them, the medicines you have already taken for the fever, and any recent reports. Bring the list of medicines you take regularly as well.

Vaccines against typhoid and flu

Some fevers can be prevented. The clinic offers the typhoid vaccine and the annual flu vaccine for adults; see adult vaccination.

When not to wait for an appointment

Confusion or unusual drowsiness, severe breathlessness, chest pain, a fit, a stiff neck with a severe headache, or bleeding are emergencies, whether the fever is high or mild. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department rather than waiting for a clinic slot.
